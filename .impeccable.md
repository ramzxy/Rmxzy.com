## Design Context

### Users

Rmxzy serves an equal mix of technical readers interested in systems, security, and software, and professional visitors such as potential employers, collaborators, and clients. Blog visitors need to discover relevant writing quickly, understand Ilia's credibility without wading through portfolio copy, and read long, code-heavy articles comfortably on desktop or mobile.

### Brand Personality

Technical, candid, and quietly confident. The site should feel authored by a curious systems engineer and security researcher, not like a generic developer template or a corporate publication. Reading should feel calm and focused while retaining Rmxzy's sharp terminal character.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a quiet editorial reading surface with subtle Rmxzy terminal details. Writing is a first-class part of the main portfolio, but the archive and articles remain low-key. Retain the existing tinted dark and light themes, phosphor accent, compact Whyte Inktrap labels, and restrained grain. Use one scroll-driven media expansion as the blog's signature entrance, inspired by 21st.dev's scroll-expansion pattern, then reduce particles, glow, custom-cursor behavior, and card-heavy layouts so typography and content lead.

### Design Principles

1. Reading comes first: keep prose near 65 characters per line, use generous line height, and make headings, code, media, and notes easy to scan.
2. Preserve identity through details, not noise: use terminal cues for navigation and metadata while keeping the article itself editorial and calm.
3. Serve technical and professional readers equally: expose depth through tags, search, archives, and code while keeping titles, summaries, and navigation understandable to newcomers.
4. Make discovery effortless: provide a strong blog index, useful filtering, reading time, table of contents, and previous/next paths without clutter.
5. Ship complete publication infrastructure: responsive behavior, keyboard access, reduced motion, metadata, structured data, RSS, sitemap coverage, and legacy URL preservation are part of the design.
6. Concentrate motion: one strong, performant scroll moment is enough; the archive and reading view should stay calm.
7. Make cover art explain the post: use one legible visual thesis, two or three conceptual elements at most, strong negative space, and the established black, cream, and rust-red halftone treatment. Reject generic cyber props, decorative circuitry, fake interfaces, and complexity that only exists to look expensive.

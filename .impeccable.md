## Design Context

### Users

Rmxzy is first a personal art object and independent internet home. It serves curious technical readers, artists, friends, collaborators, and anyone interested in systems, security, music, software, and Ilia's point of view. Professional visitors are welcome, but the experience must never be flattened into recruiter-first portfolio design.

### Brand Personality

Technical, candid, and quietly confident. The site should feel authored by a curious systems engineer and security researcher, not like a generic developer template or a corporate publication. Reading should feel calm and focused while retaining Rmxzy's sharp terminal character.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a dense DedSec-inspired publishing system rather than a generic hacker interface. The foundation is a continuous editorial evidence wall: real work, images, dates, roles, technologies, and destinations are tightly indexed and interrupted by a few original activist-print compositions. Use monochrome imagery, fluorescent overprint, halftone, hard cuts, and blunt personal copy. Keep terminal language as an easter egg, not the visible brand. Writing remains calm and legible, while the homepage is allowed to be bold, strange, visual, and unmistakably authored.

### Design Principles

1. Reading comes first: keep prose near 65 characters per line, use generous line height, and make headings, code, media, and notes easy to scan.
2. Preserve identity through details, not noise: use terminal cues for navigation and metadata while keeping the article itself editorial and calm.
3. Make art before a pitch: communicate Ilia's taste and curiosity without hiding the substance of the work.
4. Make discovery effortless: provide a strong blog index, useful filtering, reading time, table of contents, and previous/next paths without clutter.
5. Ship complete publication infrastructure: responsive behavior, keyboard access, reduced motion, metadata, structured data, RSS, sitemap coverage, and legacy URL preservation are part of the design.
6. Concentrate motion: one strong, performant scroll moment is enough; the archive and reading view should stay calm.
7. Make cover art explain the post: use one legible visual thesis, two or three conceptual elements at most, strong negative space, and the established black, cream, and rust-red halftone treatment. Reject generic cyber props, decorative circuitry, fake interfaces, and complexity that only exists to look expensive.
8. Treat color like screenprint ink: one fluorescent accent per composition, never a permanent rainbow of neon UI states.
9. Prefer continuous indexed layouts over isolated card galleries. Density should come from real information and shared edges, not from decorative widgets or clutter.
10. Every visible label must carry real information. Do not invent telemetry, revision numbers, slogans, status copy, or hacker-flavored filler merely to create atmosphere.
